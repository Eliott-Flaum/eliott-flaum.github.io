About

My work investigates subcellular structures underlying cellular dynamics in non-model organisms, focusing on the cytoskeleton in relation to morphology, behavior and division.

I am currently an EIPOD Postdoctoral Fellow at EMBL, working across the Dey Lab, UMI Lab, and Erzberger Group to study diatom cell cycle modulation underlying bloom formation, where I am investigating cell morphology dynamics and division rates in bloom-forming diatoms. As part of this project, I worked with two fellow co-first authors to adapt expansion microscopy for use in diatoms, published in Current Biology.

Previous work: I completed my Ph.D. in Biophysics at Stanford University (2024), working in the Prakash Lab in the Department of Bioengineering. My main research projects there focused on cytoskeleton, membrane and morphology dynamics in the unicellular organism Lacrymaria olor, understanding how this predatory ciliate is able to accomplish dramatic, rapid and reversible morphological dynamics as it searches for prey. We identified unique cytoskeleton and membrane architectures in the cell using high-speed, high-resolution microscopy, fluorescence microscopy, scanning electron microscopy and transmission electron microscopy, and discovered the presence of a curved-crease origami structure inside of a cell and built a physical model to replicate the structure and its dynamics. The work appeared in Science.

I received my B.A. in Physics from Northwestern University in 2017.
